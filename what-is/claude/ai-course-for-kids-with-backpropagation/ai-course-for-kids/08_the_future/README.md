# Module 8: The Future

## Teacher Script
Everything so far — predicting numbers (Learning), generating text (Creating), thinking in steps
(Reasoning), using tools and driving (Acting) — is called **Narrow AI**: very good at one type of
task. **AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)** is a hypothetical/future AI that could reason, learn,
and adapt across *any* task the way a human can. It has not been achieved, and is a topic of active
research and genuine expert disagreement.

## Kid-Friendly Analogy
"A chess AI can beat any human at chess but can't tie its shoes. A self-driving car can drive but
can't write a poem. You, a human, can do both — and learn a brand-new skill you've never tried
before, just by understanding instructions. AGI would be an AI that's flexible like that."

## Key Vocabulary
- **Narrow AI** — AI good at one specific task (everything covered in Modules 1–6)
- **AGI** — hypothetical AI with human-like general reasoning across any task
- **AI Safety** — the field of research focused on making powerful AI systems reliable and beneficial

## Activity A: Narrow vs. General Sorting (15 min)
Give students a list of 15 real AI systems (translation apps, chess engines, spam filters,
recommendation algorithms, self-driving cars, voice assistants, etc.) and have them sort into
"Narrow AI" (all of them, today) vs. "would need to be AGI" (a system that could do all 15 tasks
plus anything new thrown at it). This makes concrete that **every AI system that exists today is narrow.**

## Activity B: Class Debate — "Are We Close to AGI?" (30 min)
Split the class into two teams researching real, current expert opinions (with supervised web search):
- **Team A:** arguments that AGI could arrive soon
- **Team B:** arguments for major remaining obstacles (reasoning limits, lack of embodiment, energy
  costs, safety)

Goal isn't a "correct" answer — it's showing that even experts disagree, and that evaluating evidence
matters more than picking a side.

## Hands-On Tutorial: `tutorial.py`
A small interactive "Narrow AI Sorter" quiz script students can run and extend with their own examples.

## Data & Bias Checkpoint (Final, Cumulative)
Look back across all 7 modules: **"At every single stop, the AI was only as good as its data or its
rules. Who decided what data/rules those were? Who's responsible when it gets something wrong?"**
This closes the loop that opened in Module 1's "Whose rules are these?" question.

## Discussion Questions
- If AGI were achieved, what jobs or tasks do you think would change first?
- What rules or safety measures would you want in place *before* a general-purpose AI was given
  control of something important?
- Looking back at Rules -> Learning -> Neural Networks -> Creating -> Reasoning -> Acting: which stop
  surprised you most, and why?
