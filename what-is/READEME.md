Traditional programming and machine learning really are fundamentally different animals, 
and it's worth being precise about why before touching any code. Let me explain, then build you the real thing.

# Why "if/else" isn't AI

In traditional programming, a human writes every rule the computer will ever follow. The 
computer never deviates from what's on the page — it just executes logic a person already
figured out.

In machine learning, nobody writes the rules for the actual task. Instead, a human writes a
learning procedure. The "rules" — really just a giant pile of numbers called weights — are
discovered by the computer itself, by looking at examples and gradually nudging those
numbers until mistakes go down. Let me show you the shape of that difference:
